# 10 Questions to Ask Before Booking Your Wedding Entertainment

Before booking your wedding entertainment, ask about the performer's live-wedding experience, their backup plan if they can't perform, whether they play live or use tracks, exactly what's included in the fee, and how they coordinate with your other vendors. These five questions, expanded into a full checklist of ten, separate seasoned professionals from performers who look good on paper but haven't been tested on a real wedding day. This guide breaks each question down so you can vet any singer, musician, or band with confidence, whether you're comparing quotes or ready to sign a contract.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask about verified live-wedding experience, not just years "in the industry."

- Confirm a written backup plan for illness, emergencies, or equipment failure.

- Clarify whether the performance is fully live, and what "live" actually means for that act.

- Get a written quote that lists every cost, including travel and overtime.

- Confirm the performer can learn your specific songs, including your first dance.

- Ask how they coordinate timing with your planner, DJ, and photographer.

- Check for liability insurance, especially if your venue requires it.

- Make sure their set can adapt across ceremony, cocktail hour, and reception.

- Always get the agreement in writing, including deposit and cancellation terms.

- Verify reviews independently rather than relying on testimonials alone.

## Why a Vetting Checklist Matters for Wedding Entertainment

A vetting checklist matters because wedding entertainment cannot be redone. Unlike a florist or a rented linen, a missed cue, a canceled performer, or an unrehearsed song happens in real time, in front of every guest, and can't be fixed after the fact. Couples who ask structured questions upfront consistently report fewer day-of surprises and a smoother overall vendor experience.

The questions below come from the same due diligence process experienced planners and performers recommend before any contract is signed. Each one is designed to surface information that a polished website or a low price alone won't tell you.

- How Many Weddings Have You Actually Performed?

The direct answer: ask for a number tied to a timeframe, such as weddings performed in the last twelve months, along with a way to verify it. Years "in the music industry" is not the same as years performing live at weddings, which have their own pacing, sound requirements, and timeline pressure.

- Ask for recent, unedited video from an actual ceremony or reception.

- Ask how many weddings they perform in a typical season.

- Ask about formal training, since a strong musical foundation (such as conservatory-level training) tends to translate into better live adaptability.

- What Happens If You're Sick or Unavailable?

The direct answer: every professional performer should have a documented backup plan, whether that's a trusted substitute musician, a network of peers who can step in, or a contractual guarantee. If a performer hesitates or says it's never come up, treat that as a gap, not reassurance.

- Ask who the specific backup would be, not just that one exists.

- Ask whether backup coverage is written into the contract.

- Ask about equipment failure, not just personal emergencies.

- For a closer look at how this should work in practice, see what actually happens if your wedding musician doesn't show up.

- Are You Playing Live, or Using Tracks?

The direct answer: ask the performer to describe exactly what's live versus pre-recorded, and don't accept a vague answer. A singer-guitarist genuinely playing and singing live delivers a different atmosphere than a performer singing over a backing track, and couples deserve to know which they're paying for.

Why this matters more than it seems

Live music can adapt in real time. It can stretch a song while you finish walking down the aisle, soften the volume during a speech, or take a request from the dance floor. Tracks can't do any of that.

- What's Included in the Fee, and Are There Hidden Costs?

The direct answer: request a written, itemized quote before you book. Ask specifically about travel fees, overtime rates, additional sets (ceremony plus cocktail hour plus reception), and equipment costs like sound systems or lighting. As a benchmark, professional live wedding music in a major metro area typically runs from roughly $2,500 for ceremony-and-cocktail-hour coverage up to $7,000 or more for full-day ceremony-through-reception packages, depending on hours and add-ons.

For how those numbers split further by band, DJ, and solo format across the DMV specifically, the full band-vs-DJ-vs-solo cost breakdown shows exactly where each quote typically stops short.

- Get the total cost in writing, not just a verbal estimate.

- Ask what happens if your event runs long.

- Confirm whether travel outside a certain radius adds a fee.

- Ask what's bundled in, such as sound systems, wireless microphones for the officiant and toasts, or printed song menus, since bundling can mean one vendor instead of a separate AV rental.

- Can You Learn Our First Dance Song and Take Requests?

The direct answer: ask directly whether learning new material is included in the fee, and how much lead time they need. Most professional wedding musicians build song learning into their process, but the number of songs included and the deadline to request them varies by performer. Requests from guests on the night are a separate question, and who decides what gets played is worth settling too.

- For inspiration on what to request, browse 20 acoustic guitar songs for a wedding ceremony, covering options from processional to recessional.

- How Do You Coordinate With Our Other Vendors?

The direct answer: ask whether the performer is comfortable working directly from a timeline provided by your planner or day-of coordinator, and how they handle last-minute changes. Entertainment that can't adjust to a delayed processional or a shortened cocktail hour creates stress for everyone else on site.

- Ask if they'll join a vendor call or review the timeline in advance.

- Ask how they communicate with your DJ or band if the reception involves a handoff.

- Ask how far in advance they want your final song list and timeline.

- What Equipment Do You Bring, and Are You Insured?

The direct answer: ask what sound equipment is included, whether it's suitable for your venue size, and request a certificate of liability insurance. Many venues, especially hotels and historic properties, require proof of insurance (often $1M or more, with the venue named as additional insured) before allowing outside vendors to perform, so confirming this early avoids a last-minute scramble.

- Can Your Set Adapt Across Ceremony, Cocktail Hour, and Reception?

The direct answer: ask for examples of how their repertoire shifts across each part of the day. A ceremony calls for a different mood than cocktail hour, and cocktail hour is different again from reception entrances or a first dance.

- What's Your Contract, Deposit, and Cancellation Policy?

The direct answer: never book without a signed contract that spells out the date, time, payment schedule, deposit amount, and cancellation terms. A verbal agreement offers no protection if plans change on either side.

- Can I See Reviews or Talk to a Past Couple?

The direct answer: ask for reviews on an independent platform (Google, The Knot, WeddingWire) rather than only testimonials curated on the performer's own website, and ask if they're willing to connect you with a recent couple. Independently verifiable feedback is a stronger trust signal than curated quotes alone.

## Common Mistakes Couples Make When Booking Entertainment

- Choosing the right wedding ceremony musician in Washington, DC involves more than comparing prices. One of the biggest mistakes couples make is selecting a performer based on cost alone without looking closely at what's included in each package. Another common oversight is assuming there's a backup plan in place without asking how unexpected situations, such as illness or equipment issues, would be handled.

- It's also important to avoid relying on verbal agreements or a simple deposit instead of a written contract that clearly outlines the services, performance times, and expectations. Before booking, confirm whether the music will be performed live or with backing tracks so there are no surprises on your wedding day.

- Waiting too long to book a wedding ceremony musician in Washington, DC can mean missing out on experienced performers, especially during peak wedding season when the most sought-after musicians are often reserved months in advance. Finally, don't assume your musician will automatically coordinate with your planner, officiant, or venue. Confirming these details ahead of time helps ensure your ceremony runs seamlessly from beginning to end.

## Expert Tips for Vetting a Wedding Musician

- Book nine to twelve months out for peak season dates, and even earlier for in-demand performers.

- Ask for a short video call performance if an in-person preview isn't possible.

- Request references tied to weddings similar in size or venue type to yours.

- Confirm music training and mentorship background as an added trust signal; a strong musical education often shows up in a performer's adaptability on the day.

- Put every verbal promise (song learning, timeline flexibility, backup coverage) into the written contract.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should we book wedding entertainment?

Most couples book their wedding ceremony musician in Washington, DC six to twelve months before the wedding date, especially if they're getting married during peak wedding season (May, June, September, and October) or on a popular Saturday. Sought-after performers often book up a year or more in advance, so securing your preferred musician early gives you the best selection and helps avoid last-minute compromises.

### What's the difference between a live wedding musician and a DJ?

A live musician performs songs in real time using voice and instrument, which creates a warmer, more personal atmosphere, especially for a ceremony or cocktail hour. A DJ plays recorded tracks and can offer a wider genre range and continuous dance music. Many couples choose a hybrid: live acoustic guitar for the ceremony and cocktail hour, then a live-plus-DJ set for the reception dance floor, all from one performer and one sound system.

### Should we ask to see the performer play live before booking?

Yes, whenever possible. A live video, a public performance, or a video call performance gives a far more accurate picture than photos or a produced demo reel alone. If an in-person preview isn't practical, ask for recent, unedited video clips from actual weddings.

### What questions reveal if a wedding musician is truly experienced?

Ask about their music education and training, how many weddings they've performed in the last year, how they handle timeline changes on the day of, and whether they can name specific challenges they've navigated (weather, sound issues, last-minute song changes). Specific, confident answers, backed by verified reviews on platforms like Google, The Bash, or Thumbtack, are a stronger signal than a long list of credentials alone.
