# 20 Acoustic Guitar Songs for a Wedding Ceremony (Processional to Recessional)

Dylan Galvin is a Berklee College of Music graduate, mentored by music legends John Mayer, Paul Simon and Livingston Taylor. Performing across the United States since 2009, now based in the DMV (Washington DC, Maryland, and Virginia), more than 1,500 events, including work for Coca-Cola, Wells Fargo, Lincoln, and CBRE.

Picking the right acoustic guitar songs for a wedding ceremony comes down to matching the song to the moment. A processional needs to build anticipation without rushing anyone down the aisle. A recessional needs energy. The songs in between need to hold a room that's trying not to cry.

Below is a working list pulled from songs actually in rotation for real DC-area ceremonies, organized by the moment they're built for. Guitar with vocals is one part of what's offered for weddings, but for a ceremony specifically, instrumental fingerstyle arrangements tend to work best, since there's no PA competing with the officiant.

## Processional (walking down the aisle)

The processional sets the tone for everything that follows, soft, unhurried, and instrumental, so the moment stays about the people walking, not the music.

- "Here Comes the Sun" - The Beatles (instrumental)

- "Can't Help Falling in Love" - Elvis Presley (instrumental)

- "Your Song" - Elton John

- "Viva La Vida" - Coldplay (instrumental)

- "Norwegian Wood" - The Beatles

These work well for a ceremony because they're recognizable enough to feel intentional but restrained enough not to upstage the moment. A skilled acoustic guitarist can slow any of these to processional tempo without losing the melody.

## During the ceremony (readings, unity moments, vow exchange)

This is where the guitar needs to disappear into the background, present, but not competing with anyone speaking.

- "Real Love" - The Beatles (instrumental)

- "Stay With Me" - Sam Smith (instrumental)

- "Just the Way You Are" - Bruno Mars (instrumental)

- "Human Nature" - Michael Jackson (instrumental)

- "With or Without You" - U2 (instrumental)

An instrumental arrangement matters more here than song choice, quiet fingerstyle picking that fills silence without pulling attention from the couple.

## Why So Many of These Are Marked Instrumental

Couples ask for a vocal-and-guitar version of a meaningful song more often than any other request. Dylan's answer, in most of those cases, is to rearrange the same song for instrumental fingerstyle guitar instead of performing it with vocals. The reasoning is simple once it's pointed out: a vocal performance pulls guest attention toward the performer at the exact moment it should be on the bride. It turns the walk down the aisle into something closer to a performance than a backdrop, working against the entire reason the song was requested in the first place.

The fix isn't to talk a couple out of the song they wanted. It's to rearrange it. The melody and the meaning stay intact, played as instrumental fingerstyle guitar instead of guitar and vocals, so the song still functions as the couple wanted it to, as something that complements the moment rather than competing with it.

## Recessional (walking back up the aisle)

Recessional songs do the opposite of processional songs: build energy, signal the shift from ceremony to celebration, and get people ready to clap. This is also the one ceremony moment where a vocal version tends to work rather than compete, since the tension has already lifted and a full sung performance reads as celebration, not distraction.

- "I Want You Back" - The Jackson 5

- "Brown Eyed Girl" - Van Morrison

- "Wonderwall" - Oasis

- "1983" - John Mayer

- "Use Somebody" - Kings of Leon

These are played with more drive, full strumming instead of fingerstyle, tempo pushed up, energy visibly rising.

## Cocktail hour bonus round

Once the ceremony wraps, the job shifts again, from emotional anchor to background texture for a room full of people who now want to talk to each other over drinks.

- "Better Together" - Jack Johnson

- "I'm Yours" - Jason Mraz

- "Budapest" - George Ezra

- "Riptide" - Vance Joy

- "Copperline" - James Taylor

## How to actually pick your set

Most couples don't need to memorize this list. They need a guitarist who already knows it and can build a ceremony set around their specific readings, timing, and how many guests are walking down the aisle before them. That's the conversation worth having before the wedding, matching songs to moments, and knowing when a requested song should stay instrumental instead of adding vocals, not just handing over a playlist.
