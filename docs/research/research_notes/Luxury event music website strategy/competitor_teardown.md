## Comparison overview: structure/IA, hero, CTAs, media, copy tone

### Takeaway
Premium acts organize their sites by event type (Weddings / Corporate / Social / Charity) and lead with a credentials-based headline plus one consultation-style CTA. The strongest sites back this up with dense, specific proof: brand logos, press logos, named reviewers, review counts. Weaker ones (Élan, Bob Gail Music, Hank Lane) look under-built and generic. None of the sites examined publish dollar prices. The tone ranges from quiet and planner-facing (Bob Gail, Élan, Scarlett) to loud party-band copy (Bachelor Boys, Party on the Moon). The quiet register is the better fit for luxury galas.

## How do premium acts separate "artist/fan" content from "book us" content?

### Takeaway
They separate the two in one of three ways: a different domain (Bob Gail Planning vs. Music), a separate nav section (Party on the Moon's "FOUNDER" menu for movie, book, and podcast), or an agency act page that turns artist credentials into booking proof (Scarlett). In every case the artist identity appears as credibility (credits, co-bills, label, press), never as a fan funnel such as tour dates or merch on the booking path.

### Cited Findings
- Bob Gail splits planning and music into two domains. The services nav "Music" item links out to bobgailmusic.com. — [Bob Gail](https://www.bobgail.com/)
- Party on the Moon puts founder/artist media (The Movie, The Book, Podcast, Master Class) under a separate "FOUNDER" menu, apart from ENTERTAINMENT and CONTACT ("Book Now", "Book a Consultation"). — [Dennis Smith Ent.](https://dennissmithentertainment.com/party-bands/party-on-the-moon)
- The Scarlett page presents the artist identity ("Award-winning, LA-based violinist, music producer, and DJ"; label Enormous Tunes; a syndicated radio show on 45+ stations; shared stages with Kygo and Boyz II Men) inside a booking page as a "Biography" tab, next to "Previous Clients." — [Scarlett](https://scarlettentertainment.com/us/acts/luxury-dj-electric-violinist)
- Lost Wax files press and TV (HBO Max "Second Chance Stage", ESPN, NFL Draft) under Media > TV/Radio and Press Kit, and uses it as proof on the homepage. — [Lost Wax](https://lostwaxband.com/)

### Inferences
- For Sean, a sound pattern is: the booking site is the primary experience (event-type pages, a "Private Events" CTA in the nav), and the artist and fan material (releases, Spotify, tour, merch) lives in a separate "Music" section or subdomain. Sync placements and charting get pulled into booking pages as a short credits strip ("As heard in…").

### Gaps
- No fetched example showed a solo recording artist's personal site with a well-separated private-booking section (for example, an artist site with a /private-events page). Candidates would need more searching.

## Do they publish pricing or package structures?

### Takeaway
No luxury or agency site examined publishes dollar pricing. They structure offerings instead by ensemble size, named tier, or add-on menu, and send visitors to a consultation. Dollar figures appear only on marketplaces (The Bash "Starting at $300/$750 per event") and in agency budget filters (CTI $2,500 to $2M+). The mid-market Bachelor Boys has a "Prices & FAQ" nav item but no figures on its homepage.

### Cited Findings

- CTI budget filter: Budget Min $2,500 to $1,000,000; Budget Max $7,499 to $2,000,000+. — [CTI](https://www.celebritytalent.net/)

## Inquiry form fields, steps, availability checks, response-time promises

### Takeaway
Luxury sites favor a consultation CTA ("Set an appointment", "Book A Consultation", "Let's Talk") over a generic contact form. The most structured flows (ECE's six steps, CTI's multi-step form, Scarlett's category-plus-location modal and planner "proposal" tool) qualify by event type, location, and budget. None of the sites examined made an explicit response-time promise or offered a live availability calendar.

### Cited Findings
- ECE publishes a six-step process: share event type, location, and preferred talent; search by location; submit and connect with a consultant; brief call; receive a list curated to "preferences, budget, and availability"; contract. — [ECE](https://www.bookece.com/)
- CTI uses a multi-step form ("Go to Next Step >") with an event-type selector (Corporate Event, Endorsement Projects, Fundraising Event, Concert or Music Event, Fair or Festival, College or University, Private Event, Social Media Project). — [CTI](https://www.celebritytalent.net/)
- Scarlett's "Start Inquiry" modal offers request categories (Corporate Entertainment, Weddings, "WOW Factor", etc.) and location filters. Planners get an "Add to proposal" tool with Proposal Ref, Client Name, Proposal Owner, and Event Country. The only reply language is that the team "will be in touch," with no timeframe. — [Scarlett](https://scarlettentertainment.com/us/acts/luxury-dj-electric-violinist)
- Hank Lane: "Set an appointment" appointment-booking CTA plus a separate contact form page. — [Hank Lane](https://www.hanklane.com/)
- Lost Wax: "submit an inquiry" for date availability; follow-up with availability and pricing; no stated response time. — [Lost Wax](https://lostwaxband.com/)
- Bachelor Boys lists "24/7" "Flexible Availability," which means booking availability including last-minute bookings, not reply speed. — [Bachelor Boys](https://www.bachelorboysband.com/)
- Bob Gail Special Events offers phone and email only. — [Bob Gail](https://www.bobgail.com/)

### Inferences
- Sean can beat this whole set by stating a response-time promise ("Personal reply from Sean's team within 24 hours"), which no competitor here does, and by checking availability by date in the form's first step.
- A recommended form for Sean, synthesized from ECE, CTI, and Scarlett: Event type (Wedding / Corporate & Brand / Private & Estate / Charity Gala) → date + venue/city → guest count → ensemble (Solo / Duo / Trio / Full band, or "advise me") → moments needed (ceremony / cocktail / dinner / reception) → budget range → name, email, phone, and role (couple / planner / corporate). A separate planner/agency path should be offered, given Scarlett's planner proposal tooling.

### Gaps
- Could not see actual field lists on Hank Lane's /contact-us-form, ECE's /request_more/, Lost Wax's contact page, or CTI's Request Info form; they were not fetched within budget.

### Top takeaways for seancarneymusic.com
1. Organize the site by event type (Weddings / Corporate & Brand Activations / Private & Estate / Charity Galas) with one consultation CTA, and move fan content to a separate "Music" section or subdomain (Bob Gail, Party on the Moon pattern).

5. Use a multi-step inquiry form with a date check first, an event-type branch, and a planner path, plus a published response-time promise, which no competitor examined makes.
6. Rewrite the copy in a calm, specific, credential-led register; ALL-CAPS "high energy" reads mid-market (Bachelor Boys). Add an event reel plus short audio samples; no competitor examined uses audio.
